How to work out how many acoustic panels you need

Acoustic panels reduce echo, flutter, and reverberation inside a room—they do not soundproof walls between rooms. This guide covers both the everyday coverage-percentage method and the Sabine RT60 model.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Measure room length, width, and ceiling height in feet or meters.
  2. If you have not measured your room reverberation time, use Coverage % mode: 10% to 15% of wall area for light echo control in offices, 15% to 25% for meeting or podcast rooms, and 25% to 40% for critical studios.
  3. If you have measured your current RT60 reverberation time (for example with a measurement microphone or clap-decay app), switch to Sabine RT60 mode and enter current RT60, target RT60, and panel NRC/absorption values.
  4. Enter the dimensions of the panels you plan to hang (common broadband panels are 4 × 2 ft or 120 × 60 cm).

What to enter in the calculator

  • Calculation method: Use Coverage % for typical room planning, or Sabine when you measured RT60.
  • Room length: Room length in feet or meters.
  • Room width: Room width in feet or meters.
  • Ceiling height: Floor-to-ceiling height.
  • Target wall coverage: 10–15% light (office); 15–25% moderate (calls/podcast); 25–40% heavy (studio).
  • Measured current RT60: Measured reverberation decay time in seconds (Sabine mode).
  • Target RT60: Desired reverberation time—commonly 0.3–0.5 s studio, 0.5–0.7 s office.
  • Panel length / width: Common broadband panel is 4 × 2 ft (1.2 × 0.6 m).
  • Panel height: Second face dimension of one panel.
  • Panel absorption coefficient (α / NRC): Typical 2 in mineral wool ≈ 0.80–0.95 (Sabine mode).
  • Replaced wall absorption coefficient: Painted drywall/plaster is roughly 0.04–0.05 (Sabine mode).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying absorption panels expecting to block traffic noise or noisy neighbors. Sound isolation requires mass, sealed air gaps, and decoupled walls—not surface panels.
  • Buying thin 1-inch foam expecting bass control. Thin foam mainly absorbs high frequencies and can leave a room sounding dull yet boomy.
  • Clustering every panel on one wall. Spreading panels across opposite walls and first-reflection points prevents flutter echo far more effectively.
  • Treating a single NRC rating as equal across all frequencies—always check octave-band absorption data down to 125 Hz and 250 Hz.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Start with first-reflection points on side walls and behind speakers or desks before adding decorative panels.
  • Use 2-inch (50 mm) mineral wool or fiberglass panels for voice and general rooms, and 4-inch (100 mm) panels or corner traps for studios.
  • Leave a small air gap behind wall-mounted panels when possible to extend low-mid frequency absorption.
