How to how many acoustic panels do i need

Explore how much additional absorption a Sabine room model predicts from a measured reverberation time and a target time. It estimates room treatment, not sound isolation between rooms.

Quick answer: A 50 m³ room changing from 1.2 s to 0.6 s needs about 6.71 m² of additional absorption in this model: 13 panels providing 0.54 m² each.

What you need to measure

  • Room length
  • Room width
  • Room height
  • Measured current reverberation time
  • Target reverberation time
  • Panel width
  • Panel height
  • Panel absorption fraction
  • Replaced surface absorption fraction

How to calculate it

In SI units, Sabine’s relation is T = 0.161 × V ÷ A. Use measured current time to infer current absorption, and target time to calculate target absorption. Additional absorption is the positive difference. Divide by panel area multiplied by the difference between panel and replaced-surface absorption fractions, then round up.

What changes the estimate

Use values from the same frequency band and a manufacturer’s appropriate absorption data. A single NRC number is not a complete frequency response. Sabine assumes an approximately diffuse field and is only an estimate for small or unevenly treated rooms. Mounting, placement, furniture and low-frequency behavior need separate consideration. Example times are illustrative, not measurements of your room.

Worked example

A 50 m³ room changing from 1.2 s to 0.6 s needs about 6.71 m² of additional absorption in this model: 13 panels providing 0.54 m² each.
