How many acoustic panels do I need?

Estimate acoustic panel count using either wall-coverage percentage (when room reverberation time is unmeasured) or the Sabine RT60 room-absorption model.

Quick answer: US room coverage: a 16 × 12 × 8 ft room (448 sq ft walls) at 20% coverage needs 89.6 sq ft, or 12 standard 4 × 2 ft panels. Metric coverage: a 5 × 4 × 2.5 m room (45 m² wall area) at 20% moderate coverage needs 9.0 m² of treatment, or 13 panels of 1.2 × 0.6 m.

How the calculation works

In Coverage % mode, calculate total wall surface area, apply your target coverage percentage (10%–15% light, 15%–25% moderate, 25%–40% heavy), and divide by single-panel area. In Sabine RT60 mode, infer current and target absorption from room volume and measured reverberation time using T = 0.161 × V / A, then divide required added absorption by net absorption per panel.

Worked examples (US and metric)

Example 1

Metric coverage: a 5 × 4 × 2.5 m room (45 m² wall area) at 20% moderate coverage needs 9.0 m² of treatment, or 13 panels of 1.2 × 0.6 m.

Example 2

US room coverage: a 16 × 12 × 8 ft room (448 sq ft walls) at 20% coverage needs 89.6 sq ft, or 12 standard 4 × 2 ft panels.

Example 3

Sabine RT60 model: reducing a 50 m³ room from 1.2 s to 0.6 s with 0.80-absorption panels over 0.05 walls needs 6.71 m² added absorption (13 panels).

What this estimate does not cover

Acoustic panels reduce echo and reverberation inside the room—they do not soundproof walls between rooms. A single NRC number hides weak low-frequency absorption on thin panels. Sabine assumes a diffuse sound field; room modes, bass traps, furniture, and first-reflection placement require separate attention.
